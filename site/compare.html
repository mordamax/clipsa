Clipsa vs other clipboard managers

Clipsa is not the right clipboard manager for everyone. Here is where it is ahead, where other apps are ahead, and the sources for every claim about them.

Download Clipsa Jump to the table

Updated November 2026

Built for different people

Choose Clipsa if…

  • You want history encrypted by default, and to be able to check it. Every item is encrypted on disk with no setting to turn on. Maccy, ClipBook, Ditto and EcoPaste store history as plain SQLite (checked in their source code). CopyQ can encrypt, but it is off by default. Raycast says its history is encrypted, but its code is closed.
  • You work across macOS, Windows and Linux. Of the apps below, only CopyQ also runs on all three.
  • You want an app lock with Touch ID or Windows Hello. None of the other apps documents one. Paste's request for it is still open.
  • You want it free with no limits. No trial, no history cap, no subscription.

Pick something else if…

  • You need sync and phone apps. Paste syncs through iCloud to iPhone and iPad. Pastebot syncs between Macs. Clipsa is desktop-only and keeps history on one computer.
  • You search text in images (OCR). Maccy, Paste, ClipBook and Raycast can search text inside screenshots. Clipsa cannot yet.
  • You rely on text actions and automation. ClipBook has dozens of text transformations, Pastebot has filters and a command-line tool, CopyQ is fully scriptable. Clipsa has plain-text paste only.
  • You need a paste stack. Paste, ClipBook, Raycast and Pastebot can paste a series of items in order.

Some things we used to think set Clipsa apart are in fact standard: skipping copies that password managers mark as secret, excluding apps, plain-text paste, pinning, and working without an account. Most apps below do these too, so we don't claim them as advantages.

Recommendations

Which one should you pick?

  • Clipsa

    You want history that is encrypted without setting anything up, on any desktop system, with an optional Touch ID or Windows Hello lock, for free.

  • Paste

    You want the same history on your Mac, iPhone and iPad and don't mind a subscription.

  • Maccy

    You're on a Mac and want the simplest free app, with OCR, and don't need history encrypted on disk.

  • ClipBook or Pastebot

    You transform text all day and want built-in actions, filters and paste stacks on a Mac.

  • CopyQ

    You want to script everything and work across macOS, Windows and Linux, and can live with a more technical interface.

  • Raycast

    You already use Raycast as your launcher and its 3-month free history is enough.

On Clipsa's list for the coming releases: text recognition in images, a paste stack, text actions, and optional end-to-end encrypted sync. See the roadmap.
